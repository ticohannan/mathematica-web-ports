Vibrations of a Rectangular Membrane

The two-dimensional wave equation ∂t,tu = c2(∂x,xu + ∂y,yu) provides a simple model of a vibrating rectangular membrane. You can see the evolution of an initial profile with no initial velocity for a rectangular membrane with no deflection at the boundaries. You can set the superposition of the first four Fourier modes as initial data and you can observe the membrane as time evolves.

The original Demonstration has no Details text. External link: Wave Equation—Rectangle (Wolfram MathWorld).

Browser port of a Wolfram Demonstration. All computation runs in your browser. The surface is sampled on a fixed grid of 65 × 65 points (the original refines its grid adaptively), and the box's tick marks and the view are drawn as close to the original as the port can; drag the picture to rotate it. Where the original's formulas give no picture (all four amplitudes 0, or a box height reduced to zero or below) the port draws a white surface or a flat box; the details are in DESIGN.md.
