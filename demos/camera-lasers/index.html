Measuring Distance and Orientation Using Camera and Lasers

Distance to an object can be measured with a single camera and a laser, if the laser position relative to the camera is known. Usually the laser is pointed in the same direction as the camera and mounted a known distance from the camera. By dragging the locator, you can choose the orientation of the brown cuboid and two red lasers mounted s units to the left and right of a camera, which has a focal length λ. These lasers can measure the distance to the cuboid, as shown by the reticule on the camera image. The cuboid's angle can be computed from two distance measurements.

Details

In a pinhole camera, light enters the camera through a tiny hole and illuminates the image plane. A pinhole camera has a focal length λ, the distance from the pinhole to the image plane. The pinhole is shown with a blue point and the image plane as a blue line. 3D points in the camera's coordinate frame (xc, yc, zc)T are converted to image plane coordinates (u, v) according to the formula:

k (xc, yc, zc)T = (u, v, λ)T, where k = λ/zc.

All lines parallel to the camera frame's z axis terminate at z = ∞ at the vanishing point (0, 0) in the image plane.

Lasers mounted at (s, 0, 0)T and (−s, 0, 0)T that terminate at z coordinates z1 and z2 have image plane coordinates (s λ/z1, 0) and (s λ/z2, 0).

Thus the distance to objects with image plane coordinates (u1, 0) and (u2, 0) gives camera frame coordinates (s, 0, λ/u1)T and (−s, 0, λ/u2)T.

If both lasers strike the same object, the angle to the object is computed as arctan(2s, λ/u1 − λ/u2).

A camera's reticule (or graticule) is a net of fine lines in the scope. In this case, the reticule is aligned with the vanishing point of the lasers. The readings on the reticule directly measure the distance from the object to the camera.

Browser port of a Wolfram Demonstration. Drag in the top view to move the object (a press anywhere in the top view moves it there); click or drag on the dial to turn it in steps of π/32. Arrow keys move the focused object locator, and turn the focused dial by π/32 (Shift: π/8). All computation runs in your browser.
