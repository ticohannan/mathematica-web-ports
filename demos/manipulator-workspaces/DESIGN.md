## 1. Purpose and background

A teaching tool for the reachable workspace of three-joint serial robots. The user picks one of 14 robot designs
(Denavit–Hartenberg tables, the same as in "Robot Singularities in Three-Link Manipulators" minus the two wrists),
sets the range of each joint with a two-thumb range slider (r₁, r₂, r₃) and the joint positions inside those ranges
with three sliders. The workspace is not computed as a volume: it is drawn as 9 translucent light-blue parametric
surfaces — for each joint i, the surfaces where joint i sits at the minimum, the maximum and the middle of its range
while the other two joints sweep their ranges (Details: "The workspaces are generated by combining multiple 2D
parametric plots"). The robot (the same joint drawing as in Robot Singularities) is always shown on a brown ground
disk. The workspace is recomputed only when the robot or a range changes ("recalculating the workspace is slow, so
only do this when needed").

## 2. Scope

In scope: the original's controls (joint slider grid, three IntervalSliders, robot popup menu), the 14 robots, the
forward kinematics, the 9-surface workspace with its mesh and boundary lines, the robot drawing, mouse rotation, and the
original's quirks (joint reset on robot change, recompute only on change, tolerant comparisons, negative prismatic
ranges, the Details text as written).
Out of scope (would need a design entry first): a true swept volume, joint values as numbers, other robots, the
spherical wrists of Robot Singularities, inverse kinematics.

## 6. Design

- **Shared DH family (COMMON-DECISIONS §3, owner dh-parameters, the first approved of the DH apps).** The generic part
  of robot-singularities `robots.js` (`DOF`, `joints`, `dhTransform`, `jointTransform`, `makeO3coords`, `computeAd`,
  `computeTd`) becomes `demos/common/dh-chain.js`; `dhTransform` uses `Math.cos/sin` by default and exact values only
  through an explicit exact-angle argument — this app passes the table's exact angles (`Rational[±1, 2] Pi`, `Pi`,
  `0`) through it, as the stored Type rows are exact (raw notebook, snapshot Settings), and pm through the machine path
  (as implemented: `jointTransform`'s table-constant rule, which gives the same exact values, N-MW-07);
  `computeTd(Ad, {chop: true, fma: true})` (Q-MW-09, v0.1.17). `drawZArrow`, `drawCoordAxes`, `GROUND`, `drawJoint` (variant `'rs'`, the
  text of this original, identical to robot-singularities', N-MW-02) and `drawWristCenter` come from
  `demos/common/robot-draw.js`; the Graphics3D data model from `shared/graphics3d.js`; the primitive renderer
  (`addPrims`, `disposeGroup(groups)`, `fitViewer(viewer, vp)`) from `shared/graphics3d-three.js`. robot-singularities
  keeps re-export shims at its old paths, its full unit/golden/e2e suite gates the move (dh-parameters' design §6.5).
  robot-singularities keeps its `TYPES`, `TYPE_NAMES`, Jacobians, `wrapParams`, `sliderSpec`, `phaseOpt`.
- **ParametricPlot3D "Speed" sampling in the app (D-MW-10).** The draft asked dh-parameters for `ParametricPlot3D`
  options in `shared/graphics3d.js` (`Mesh -> 1`, `MeshStyle`, `BoundaryStyle`, `PerformanceGoal -> "Speed"`); the
  DH-family move added none of them (DH-FAMILY-API note), so the sampling lives in this app's `model.js`: the pure
  `sampleSpeedSurface(f)` returns the 11 × 11 grid (parameters `SPEED_PARAMS` = k/9 and 1/2), the 100 quads, the two
  mesh polylines (a = 1/2, b = 1/2) and the closed 41-point boundary, so that the renderer and the tests use the same
  samples; `workspace3D` writes them as Graphics3D data with the saved graphic's structure (per surface
  {EdgeForm[], LightBlue, Opacity[.5], Polygon…}, nine Blue boundary lines, two GrayLevel[0.2] mesh groups, its own
  option Axes -> True). `Cuboid` corners in any order are handled by g3d's `cuboidCorners` (dh-parameters N-DH-06;
  K-MW-03). Moving the sampling into `g3d.js` is left to its owner.
- **`model.js`** (pure, no DOM): `TYPES`, `TYPE_NAMES` (14 rows, the popup order and names), `initialState()` (saved
  state), `initialSettings()` (stored specifications), `selectType(st, iType)` (Q-MW-01), `jointLabels`, `pmOf(st)`,
  `workspaceSurfaces(Type, r1, r2, r3)` (the 9 functions), `sampleSpeedSurface`, `workspace3D`, `showFirstOptions`
  (K-MW-02), `fitCamera` / `groundWidthFraction` (framing, D-MW-04), `typeIndexOfStored` (golden data), `evaluate(st, cache)`: (1) pm; (2) joints of Type; (3) the recompute test with
  `mUnequal` on TypeOld/Type (compared by robot index, all 14 tables being pairwise different, unit test) and on the
  range lists, updating TypeOld and the r…old values; (4) Ad and Td from pm; (5) the two graphics as g3d data
  (`Show` keeping only the first graphic's options). It returns `{state, view, cache}` with the flags `recomputed` (the
  original's If fired) and `computed` (the port sampled the surfaces: a recompute, or a cache miss at the opening and
  after "Initial settings", D-MW-08); the workspace data are cached under the key (TypeOld, r1old, r2old, r3old), as the
  original keeps `workspace3D`. Every value change of a range thumb during a drag is one evaluation (N-MW-08).
- **State model** (Manipulate variables): `Type` (index 1…14 standing for the DH table), `TypeOld`, `params[3]`,
  `r1, r2, r3`, `r1old, r2old, r3old`; `dof = 3` is a constant.
- **`main.js`** (v0.1.21) is one `Manipulate({…})` call of `shared/manipulate.js`: controls in the original's order
  with `ControlPlacement -> Left` — the joint grid as a `Control` (built with `shared/ui.js`
  `Controls.slider({labeled: false, plus: false, steps: 'mma', min: 0, max: 1, step: 0.01})` — no value field, no ⊕
  panel, values 0 + k·0.01 as Mathematica's discrete slider; rebuilt in `after` when the robot changes), `Delimiter`,
  three IntervalSlider specs (`Method -> "Push"`, `MinIntervalSize -> 0.1`, `look: 'mma'`; `Moved` → values set by a
  reset or setState are machine reals), `Delimiter`, the popup (`PopupMenu`; `Assign` = `selectType`, which resets
  the joints, Q-MW-01); `initial` = the live state, `initialSettings` = the elbow arm (K-MW-04); `merge` = the hook's
  setState rule. The body is `evaluate` with its workspace cache; the picture is `model.js` `drawnGraphic(view)`:
  `Graphics3D[{Group3D[robot], Group3D[workspace, key]}]` — the workspace part (each surface a `GraphicsComplex` of
  its 11 × 11 grid, drawn as one indexed mesh with smooth vertex normals — the saved graphic carries VertexNormals —
  LightBlue, opacity 0.5, both faces, no depth writes, and the boundary and mesh polylines as 1 px lines, D-MW-05) is
  redrawn only when the workspace changes. `View3D` (`shared/graphics3d-view.js`) draws it with
  `outputColorSpace = LinearSRGBColorSpace` and the lights of `shared/mma-lighting.js`, framing as
  robot-singularities' workspace (SphericalRegion fit, zoom 1.35, D-MW-04), refitted only when the bounds change, the
  viewing direction kept (D-MW-07); `resetView`: Initial settings also restores the view.
- **`shared/ui-interval.js`** (MIT, generic; owned by four-bar-linkage, COMMON-DECISIONS §6; used unchanged with `labeled: false` and `look: 'mma'`): `addIntervalSlider(controls, {name,
  label, min, max, step, steps: 'mma', value: [lo, hi], minIntervalSize, method: 'Push' | 'Stop', labeled: false, onInput})`; DOM: a track, the highlighted interval, two thumb
  buttons (lower thumb below, upper thumb above the track as in the snapshots); pointer drag on a thumb snaps to
  min + k·step, clamps to [min, max], applies Push (the other thumb is moved to keep `minIntervalSize`; at a range end both
  stop) or Stop; dragging the bar between the thumbs moves both by whole steps keeping the width (lead K-MW-01: to be
  confirmed in Mathematica); one `onInput([lo, hi])` per animation frame; arrow keys on a focused thumb (A-MW-02).
  Pure helper `intervalStep(value, which, target, spec)` exported for unit tests.
- **Numerics**: tolerant comparisons from `shared/mma.js` (`mUnequal` on lists); Td chopped and multiplied with the fused machine Dot `mul4Fma` (Q-MW-09, v0.1.17); exact
  Cos/Sin of the table angles (dh-chain.js table-constant rule of `jointTransform`, N-MW-07); pm and the surface parameters `r[[1]] + t (r[[2]] - r[[1]])` in that order;

- **Evaluation order**: exactly the body's: pm, joint parameters, recompute test (and workspace), Ad, Td, Show.
