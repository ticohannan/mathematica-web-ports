Rotating a Hypercube in 4D

This Demonstration gives a variety of animated rotations of a hypercube in 4D projected to 3D. The axis (set of fixed points) in a 4D rotation is a plane. As a simple example, stop the animation and set all the angles to zero. Drag the first slider; this rotates the hypercube without distortion about the W-X plane, which we see in 3D as a rotation about the X axis.

Details

The original has no details section. Its related links: "Projections of the Four-Cube", "Rotating a Hypercube" and "Rotating Squares, Cubes, and Higher-Dimensional Hypercubes" (Wolfram Demonstrations Project).

How the picture is made: the 16 vertices (±1, ±1, ±1, ±1) are turned by the six plane rotations, moved by 2 along w and divided by 1 + pw (w + 2). The control labelled "stereographic projection" therefore sets a central (perspective) projection from the point w = −2 − 1/pw onto the hyperplane w = −2; as pw goes to 0 it becomes a parallel projection.

Browser port of a Wolfram Demonstration. All computation runs in your browser. Differences from the original: the translucent faces are blended in an order computed for the opening view (Mathematica sorts them for every view); the duration value is shown in an editable field; the rotation controls are laid out as rows instead of a grid.
