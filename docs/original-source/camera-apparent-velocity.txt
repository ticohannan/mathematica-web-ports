### [Title]
Maximizing Apparent Velocity in a Camera's Frame

### [Section]
Caption<<PaneSelectorBox>>

### [Text]
How should a drone move to maximize its apparent velocity in the frame of a camera? This Demonstration solves this problem for a drone that can move a distance r in any direction.

### [Input] (init)
(*this function shows x, y, z, Theta and Phi for the optimum distance point*)
computeShortestPt[xc_,yc_,zc_,r_]:=Module[{x,y,z,Theta,Phi},
(*Theta is the angle from +ve x axis where the optimum value occurred*)
If[xc==0\[And]yc==0,Theta=0,Theta=N[ArcTan[xc,yc]]];(*Phi is the angle from +ve z axis where the optimum value occurred*)
Phi=(3π)/(2)-N[ArcTan[Sqrt[r^2],Sqrt[xc^2+zc^2+yc^2-r^2]]+ArcTan[Sqrt[xc^2+yc^2],zc]];
(*sphere parametric equations*)
x=Chop[xc+r Cos[Theta]Sin[Phi]];
y=Chop[yc+r Sin[Theta]Sin[Phi]];
z=Chop[zc+r Cos[Phi]];
Return[{Theta,Phi,x,y,z}]
]

imagePlaneCoor[unusedvariabl_,xc_,yc_,zc_,r_]:=Module[{x,y,z,Theta,Phi,vari,vali,maxdist,λ=5},

### [Text]
If a drone (represented as a particle) can move with equal velocity in any direction, the set of possible 3D coordinates after one time step is a 3D sphere. The image plane of a camera projects all 3D points into a 2D camera frame[1]. If the sphere is centered at (Subscript[x, c],Subscript[y, c],Subscript[z, c]) with radius r and camera focal length λ, then the optimal goal location (x,y,z) for the drone is parameterized by the (latitude, longitude) pair (Phi,Theta):

### [Text]
Phi=(3π)/(2)-arctan(Sqrt[r^2],Sqrt[Subscript[x, c]^2+Subscript[y, c]^2+Subscript[z, c]^2-r^2])+arctan(Sqrt[Subscript[x, c]^2+Subscript[y, c]^2],Subscript[z, c]),

### [Text]
Theta=arctan(Subscript[x, c],Subscript[y, c]).

### [Text]
If (Subscript[x, c],Subscript[y, c]) is (0,0), there are infinite solutions with Theta∈([0,360])^\[SmallCircle].

### [Text]
The goal location is:

### [Text]
x=Subscript[x, c]+r cos Theta sin Phi

### [Text]
y=Subscript[y, c]+r sin Theta sin Phi

### [Text]
z=Subscript[z, c]+r cos Phi

### [Text]
This projects onto the image plane at the point (λ)/(z)(x,y). Because cos Phi is always negative, the drone decreases the distance in z. Interestingly, the sphere projects into a 2D ellipse in the image plane. With equations given by[2], the ellipse is centered at

### [Text]
(x (λ)/(z)-rλ Sqrt[(Subscript[x, c]^2+Subscript[y, c]^2+Subscript[z, c]^2-r^2)/((r^2-Subscript[z, c]^2)^2)]cos(arctan((y)/(x))), y(λ)/(z)-rλ Sqrt[(Subscript[x, c]^2+Subscript[y, c]^2+Subscript[z, c]^2-r^2)/((r^2-Subscript[z, c]^2)^2)]sin(arctan((y)/(x)))),

### [Text]
