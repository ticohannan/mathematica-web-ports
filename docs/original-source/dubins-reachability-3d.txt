### [Title]
3D Reachability Set for a Dubins Car

### [Section]
Caption<<PaneSelectorBox>>

### [Text]
This Demonstration shows the set of x, y, Theta positions reachable at time t by a forward-moving car with a bounded velocity and a minimum turning radius r. This set is bounded by six parametric surfaces that correspond to the six types of optimal shortest paths: RSR, LSL, RSL, LSR, RLR, LRL. Here R stands for turning right at the maximum rate, L for turning left at the maximum rate and S for moving straight. Change the dimension to switch between a 2D projection of the set with an animated car and the full 3D set with a sphere at the car's final position and orientation.

### [Input] (init)
mLS3D[τ_,t_,r_]:=Module[{Alpha=(τ t)/(r)},{-r(1-Cos[Alpha])+t (τ-1) Sin[Alpha],r Sin[Alpha]-t (τ-1) Cos[Alpha],π/2 +Alpha}]

### [Input] (init)
mRL3D[τ_,t_,r_]:=Module[{Alpha=(τ t)/(r),Beta=((1-τ) t)/(r)},{r(1-2 Cos[Alpha]+Cos[Alpha-Beta]),r(2 Sin[Alpha]-Sin[Alpha-Beta]),π/2 -Alpha+Beta}]

### [Input] (init)
mLR3D[τ_,t_,r_]:=Module[{Alpha=(τ t)/(r),Beta=((1-τ) t)/(r)}, {r(-1+2 Cos[Alpha]- Cos[Alpha-Beta]),r(2 Sin[Alpha]-Sin[Alpha-Beta]),π/2 +Alpha-Beta}]

### [Input] (init)
mRLR3D[τ_,t_,r_]:={2 r (Cos[(t τ)/(r)]-Cos[(t-2 t τ)/(2 r)]),2 r (Sin[(t τ)/(r)]+Sin[(t-2 t τ)/(2 r)]),(π)/(2)}

### [Input] (init)
mSL3D[τ_,t_,r_]:={-2 r Sin[(t (1-τ))/(2 r)]^2,t τ+2 r Cos[(t (1-τ))/(2 r)] Sin[(t (1-τ))/(2 r)],(π)/(2)+(t (1-τ))/(r)}

### [Input] (init)
mSR3D[τ_,t_,r_]:={2 r Sin[(t (1-τ))/(2 r)]^2,t τ+2 r Cos[(t (1-τ))/(2 r)] Sin[(t (1-τ))/(2 r)],(π)/(2)-(t (1-τ))/(r)}

### [Input] (init)
(*Dubbins movements: CCC, i.e. with no S (straight) parts *)
dubbins3D[τ1_,τ2_,t_,r_,p1_,p2_,p3_]:=Module[{Alpha1=(τ1 t)/(r), Alpha2=( (τ2-τ1)t)/(r),Alpha3=( (1-τ2)t)/(r) ,x,y}, 
{p1 r (-1+Cos[Alpha1])-2 p3 r Sin[(p3 Alpha3)/(2)] Sin[p1 Alpha1+p2 Alpha2+(p3 Alpha3)/(2)] + p2 r (-Cos[p1 Alpha1]+Cos[p1 Alpha1+p2 Alpha2]),
r (Sin[Alpha1]+2 p3 Cos[p1 Alpha1+p2 Alpha2+(p3 Alpha3)/(2)] Sin[(p3 Alpha3)/(2)])+p2 r (-Sin[p1 Alpha1]+Sin[p1 Alpha1+p2 Alpha2]),
π/2 + p1 Alpha1+p2 Alpha2+p3 Alpha3}
]

### [Input] (init)
(*Dubbins movements: Curve Straight Curve movements*)
dubbins3Ds[τ1_,τ2_,t_,r_,p1_,p2_,p3_]:=Module[{Alpha1=(τ1 t)/(r),Alpha3=( (1-τ2)t)/(r) ,x,y}, 
{p1 r (-1+Cos[Alpha1])-2 p3 r Sin[(p3 Alpha3)/(2)] Sin[p1 Alpha1+(p3 Alpha3)/(2)] +t (τ1-τ2) Sin[p1 Alpha1],
r (Sin[Alpha1]+2 p3 Cos[p1 Alpha1+(p3 Alpha3)/(2)] Sin[(p3 Alpha3)/(2)])+t (-τ1+τ2) Cos[p1 Alpha1],
π/2 + p1 Alpha1+p3 Alpha3}
]

### [Text]
The Dubins car is a simplified mathematical model of a car that moves in the x-y plane[1]. The car's location is specified by the (x,y) location of the center of the car's rear axle and the orientation Theta of the car. The car cannot move sideways because the rear wheels would have to slide instead of roll. The Dubins car model stipulates that the car moves forward at a constant speed and has a maximum steering angle that translates into a minimum turning radius Subscript[r, min]. In the 2D plot, the minimum turning radius circles are drawn tangent to the starting and ending positions with dashed gray circles.

### [Text]
\<If the car has forward velocity of 1 unit per second, the system equations are\>

### [Text]
dx=cos Theta,

### [Text]
dy=sin Theta,

### [Text]
dTheta=u,

### [Text]
where u∈[-1/Subscript[r, min],1/Subscript[r, min]]. The task is to minimize the length of the curve traced out by the center of the rear axle as it moves from the start to the goal. That path is the bounded-curvature shortest path. Dubins proved that the shortest path consists of no more than three segments, where in each segment u is -1/Subscript[r, min], 0 or 1/Subscript[r, min]. Denote these steering commands by R, S, L for turning right, going straight or turning left. A path is then coded with a three-letter word. The car follows the first command until switching time t*Subscript[τ, 1], the second command until t*Subscript[τ, 1] and the third command until the final time t.

### [Text]
If Subscript[r, min]=0, then the shortest path is a straight line. For nonzero Subscript[r, min], the shortest paths are of the form RSR, LSL, RSL, LSR, RLR, LRL. In this Demonstration, each path type and the corresponding parametric surface are given a different color:

### [Text]
RSR is light orange,

### [Text]
LSL is light blue,

### [Text]
RSL is green,

### [Text]
LSR brick red,

### [Text]
RLR is purple,

### [Text]
LRL is dark orange.

### [Text]
These surfaces are divided by seven 3D lines:

### [Text]
\<RS divides RSR and RSL and also bounds the 2D set from the outside right.\>

### [Text]
\<LS divides LSR and LSL and also bounds the 2D set from the outside left.\>

### [Text]
RL divides RSL from LRL for negative Theta and RLR for positive Theta. It also lower bounds the 2D set to the left.

### [Text]
LR divides LSR from LRL for negative Theta and RLR for positive Theta. It also lower bounds the 2D set to the right.

### [Text]
For RLR, ΔTheta=0 are all CCC paths that have no net change on Theta (so Subscript[τ, 1]+1-Subscript[τ, 2]=Subscript[τ, 2]-Subscript[τ, 1]). For LRL, ΔTheta=0 gives the same line. These lines divide the optimal parametric surfaces for RLR and LRL. RLR and LRL overlap, but are optimal bounds if Subscript[τ, 1]∈[0,1/2] and Subscript[τ, 2]∈[1/2,1].

### [Text]
SL divides LSL from RSL for positive Theta.

### [Text]
SR divides RSR from LSR for negative Theta.

### [Text]
See[2] for details about the 3D set reachable in time t.
