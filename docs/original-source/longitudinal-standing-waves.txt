
### [Title]
Longitudinal Standing Waves

### [Section]
Caption<<PaneSelectorBox>>

### [Text]
\<A sound wave in an organ pipe can be understood as the superposition of waves reflecting back and forth between the two ends, with destructive interference at nodes and constructive interference at antinodes.\>

### [Text]
\<Click to select the harmonic and to switch between "open" and "closed" pipes; use the "time" play/pause button to start/stop the animation; select the options to display: standing wave, wave moving to the right, wave reflected back to the left, longitudinal wave pressure contour lines, longitudinal wave densities, corresponding transverse wave, longitudinal wave "envelope" (maximum displacements of the air molecules at various positions), corresponding transverse wave envelope, and labels for displacement and/or pressure nodes and antinodes.\>

### [Text]
\<Since sound waves are longitudinal waves, the average motion of the air molecules is back and forth parallel to the direction the wave travels (the transverse visualizations are only included for comparison.)\>

(* create a bunch of spread out points, deleting near duplicates: *)
pts=Union[Transpose@{RandomReal[{-0.8(2L),L+0.8(2L)},#],RandomReal[{-H,H},#]}&@5000,SameTest->(EuclideanDistance[#1,#2]<.1&)];
(* x-coordinates for a bunch of spaced out vertical lines: *)
lineData=Range[-0.8(2L),L+0.8(2L),.01L];
lop[l_List] := Select[l,0<=#<=L&]
(* function to throw away out of range x-coordinates *)
lopPoints[l_List] := Select[l,0<=#[[1,1]]<=L&] ;(* function to throw away Points with out of range x-coordinates *)
(* functions to graph: waves moving right|left *)
fRight[x_,t_]:=Cos[2π(x/λ-t/T)];
fLeft[x_,t_]:= Cos[2π(x/λ+t/T)];
(* function to draw an arrow unless x-coords are too close, then a point: *)
myArrow[{x1_,x2_},y_]:=
Switch[Norm[x2-x1],
_?(#<.1&),Point[{(x1+x2)/2,y}],
_,Arrow[{{x1,y},{x2,y}},ε]];
(* build "\<longitudinal envelope\>", a list of arrows (or points) showing relative sizes of displacements: *)
Clear[hArrows];
hArrows[n_,L_,λ_,end_] := hArrows[n,L,λ,end] =If[end=="\<none\>",{},{Gray,Arrowheads[{-ε,ε}],
Outer[myArrow[#2,#1]&,
H Range[-.8,.8,.4] (* y-coordinates *), 
Partition[
(* massaged x-coordinates between which to draw the arrows: *)
Join[#-0.075λ Cos[(4π #)/(λ)+(π)/(2)]&/@Range[0.0,L+ε λ,λ/If[end=="\<closed\>"&&n==1,26,If[n<=2,14,If[n<=5,10,6]]]],If[end=="\<closed\>" (* add one extra end coordinate *),{L},{}]],
2,1],
1]}];
dlabels[n_,L_,λ_,end_] := dlabels[n,L,λ,end]={{Text[Subscript["\<N\>","\<d\>"],#]& /@ Table[{x,.8},{x,λ/4,L,λ/2}]},
{Text[Subscript["\<A\>","\<d\>"],#]& /@ Table[{x,.8},{x,0,L,λ/2}]}};
plabels[n_,L_,λ_,end_]:= plabels[n,L,λ,end]={{Text[Subscript["\<A\>","\<p\>"],#]& /@ Table[{x,-.85},{x,λ/4,L,λ/2}]},
{Text[Subscript["\<N\>","\<p\>"],#]& /@ Table[{x,-.85},{x,0,L,λ/2}]}};
transverseIcon=Plot[Cos[t],{t,0,3π},PlotRange->All,ImageSize->19,Axes->False];
pipecaps={{},Line[{{L+ε,-H-ε},{L+ε,H+ε}}],
Line[Partition[Table[{L+0.04(1-(-1)^i),(-1+(2/12)i)(H+2ε)},{i,0,12}],2,1]]};

### [Text]
\<This Demonstration allows experimentation with standing waves in open and closed pipes, a commonly observed type of longitudinal wave motion[1]. Longitudinal waves (including sound waves, compression/expansion waves in a Slinky, and primary earthquake P waves) propagate through a solid, liquid or gas with a speed dependent on the density of the medium.\>

### [Text]
\<Wave reflection occurs at both closed and open pipe ends, with maximum displacement (motion) possible at an open end and minimum displacement possible at a closed end. Thus there is a displacement node at any closed end and an antinode at any open end.\>

### [Text]
\<This treatment makes a number of simplifications and ignores edge effects, assuming that there is a pressure node (and displacement antinode) at a pipe's open end and a pressure antinode (and displacement node) at a closed end. In a physical pipe, the position of the pressure node at the open end varies slightly, depending on the tube diameter. The left/right displacements and the arrows showing maximum displacements are also exaggerated for easier viewing and are not intended to show the actual motion of the air, only giving an idea of relative magnitudes of the drift velocities of the molecules at different positions in the pipe.\>

### [Text]
In an open pipe of length L, the only possible wavelengths forming standing waves are those such that L is an integer multiple of the half-wavelength, while in a closed pipe, L must be an odd integer multiple of the quarter-wavelength.

### [Text]
\<The main controls are the "harmonic n" buttons to select one of the allowed wavelengths to display, the "pipe end" buttons to specify that the right end of the pipe be "open" or "closed" ("none" means the pipe goes on forever and no reflected wave, no interference, no standing wave will occur) and the "time" controls to start/stop, speed up or slow down the animation.\>

### [Text]
\<The first three option buttons toggle the display of three waves: the standing wave itself and its rightward-moving and leftward-moving components. The next three buttons specify the visual representation: pressure contour lines (vertical lines whose spacings indicate differing pressures), density (scattered points that move in response to the wave motion and so give an indication of molecular displacements and air density) and transverse (included for comparison and easy identification of wave features). The next two buttons display wave envelopes and the last two add node/antinode labels.\>

### [Text]
\<A nice introductory exploration is to display an animated rightward-moving wave in various representations, add the leftward-moving reflection and then the standing wave, seeing how they interrelate. Add an "envelope"\[LongDash]showing the maximum displacement (left/right for longitudinal, up/down for the transverse visualization) and see how the air molecules alternately move left and right, clumping at displacement node positions (pressure antinode positions) whose labels can be toggled on/off.\>

### [Text]
\<Adjust the time controls to slow down the animation enough so it evolves smoothly, not jumping. The best speed will vary depending on the number of options simultaneously shown.\>
