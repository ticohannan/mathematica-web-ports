
### [Title]
Lowpass Filter Design by Pole Placement

### [Section]
Caption<<PaneSelectorBox>>

### [Text]
An ideal lowpass filter, drawn in blue, passes all frequencies less than Subscript[Omega, c] and rejects all frequencies greater than Subscript[Omega, c]. Such a filter cannot be implemented physically because it is non-causal. However, we can design causal filters, drawn in orange, that approximate the ideal lowpass filter. This Demonstration lets you design such a filter by locating poles (of the transfer function), drawn with red ×s, in different arrangements.

### [Section]
Initialization Code<<PaneSelectorBox>>

### [CodeText]
If you provide initialization code, include a SaveDefinitions->True option in the Manipulate.

### [Section]
Manipulate<<PaneSelectorBox>>

### [Input]
Manipulate[
Module[{Theta,Hfilter,poles,imV,s},
Theta = (π)/(n);(*angle for Butterworth or elliptical*)
If[Length[p]<1,p ={ {Alpha,0}}];
(*Enabled->False*)
If[type=="\<triangle\>",
poles=Table[imV =If[n==1,0,(i-1)2/(n-1)-1];
 {Alpha(1-Abs[imV]*.95),imV},{i,1,n}]];
If[type=="\<vertical line\>",
poles=Table[{Alpha,If[n==1,0,(i-1)2/(n-1)-1]},{i,1,n}]];
If[type=="\<Butterworth\>",
poles=Table[{ Cos[π/2 +(i-1/2)Theta],Sin[π/2 +(i-1/2)Theta]},{i,1,n}]];
If[type=="\<elliptical\>",
poles=Table[{-Alpha Cos[π/2 +(i-1/2)Theta],Sin[π/2 +(i-1/2)Theta]},{i,1,n}]];
If[type=="\<manual\>",poles=p;n = Length[p];Alpha=Min[poles[[;;,1]]],p = poles];

(*generate filter based on poles*)
Hfilter[Omega_]:=Times@@Table[(1)/(\[ImaginaryJ] Omega - poles[[i,1]]-\[ImaginaryJ] poles[[i,2]]),{i,1,Length[poles]}];
(*scale filter*)
s =Max[Abs[ Hfilter[0]],Abs[ Hfilter[1]],Abs[ Hfilter[poles[[Ceiling[n/2],2]]]]];
Row[{(*plot pole positions*)
ListPlot[poles,AspectRatio->1,PlotRange->{{-2.1,0.2},{-1.1,1.1}},PlotMarkers->{Graphics[{Red,Thick,Line[{{-0.5,-0.5},{0.5,0.5}}],Line[{{-0.5,0.5},{0.5,-0.5}}]}],0.06},ImageSize->{220,350},
Ticks->{{{-2,"\<-2\!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"},{-3/2,"\<-\!\(\*FractionBox[\(3\), \(2\)]\)\!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"},{-1,"\<-\!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"},{-1/2,"\<-\!\(\*FractionBox[\(1\), \(2\)]\)\!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"}},{{-1,"\<-\[ImaginaryJ] \!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"},{-1/2,"\<-\!\(\*FractionBox[\(1\), \(2\)]\)\[ImaginaryJ] \!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"},{1/2,"\<\!\(\*FractionBox[\(1\), \(2\)]\)\[ImaginaryJ] \!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"},{1,"\<\[ImaginaryJ] \!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"}}}
],(*plot the filter in the frequency domain*)
Plot[{HeavisidePi[Omega/2],Abs[Hfilter[Omega]/s]},{Omega,-2.5,2.5},PlotRange->All,AxesLabel->{"\<Omega\>",Abs@H[Omega]},ImageSize->{350,350},ExclusionsStyle->Dashed,
Ticks->{{{-2,"\<-2\!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"},{-1,"\<-\!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"},{1,"\<\!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"},{2,"\<2\!\(\*SubscriptBox[\(Omega\), \(c\)]\)\>"}},Automatic}
]
}]],(*setup controls*)
{type,{"\<vertical line\>","\<triangle\>","\<Butterworth\>","\<elliptical\>","\<manual\>"}},
{{n,3,Row[{Style["\<n\>",Italic], "\<, number of poles\>"}]},1,20,1,Appearance->"\<Labeled\>",Enabled->If[type== "\<manual\>",False,True]},
{{Alpha,-1,"\<Alpha, real component\>"},-2,-1/100,Appearance->"\<Labeled\>",Enabled->If[type=="\<Butterworth\>"||type== "\<manual\>",False,True]},
{{p,{{-1,-1},{-1,1}}},Locator,LocatorAutoCreate->True,Appearance->None},ControlPlacement->Top
]

Details & Citations<<PaneSelectorBox>>

### [Text]
\<Four options are given for filter design by pole placement. All filters are scaled so that the maximum magnitude is about 1.\>

### [Text]
The first, "vertical line", places n poles equally spaced on the imaginary axis between -\[ImaginaryJ] Subscript[Omega, c] and \[ImaginaryJ] Subscript[Omega, c], with real component Alpha. The associated magnitude spectrum favors frequencies in the -Subscript[Omega, c] to Subscript[Omega, c] range, but the pattern has undesirable resonances at frequencies corresponding to the pole locations along the complex axis.

### [Text]
The second, "triangle", places n poles equally spaced along the imaginary axis between -\[ImaginaryJ] Subscript[Omega, c] and \[ImaginaryJ] Subscript[Omega, c], with real component Alpha(1-0.95Im(p)), where Im(p) is the imaginary component of the pole. The associated magnitude spectrum again favors frequencies in the -Subscript[Omega, c] to Subscript[Omega, c] range, but the pattern has undesirable resonances at -Subscript[Omega, c] and Subscript[Omega, c].

### [Text]
The "Butterworth" filter, introduced in 1930 by British engineer and physicist Stephen Butterworth, places the poles along the circumference of a circle of radius Subscript[Omega, c] in the left half-plane. The resulting filter is causal, BIBO stable, and flat for low frequencies. The roll-off rate is -20n dB/decade.

### [Text]
Switching to placing poles along an "elliptical" circumference enables faster roll-off, but introduces ripple in the low-frequency range.

### [Text]
\<Finally, in "manual" mode, you can drag, add, or delete poles and see the resulting effects in the frequency domain.\>
