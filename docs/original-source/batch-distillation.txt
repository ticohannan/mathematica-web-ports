
### [Title]
Batch Distillation

### [Section]
Caption<<PaneSelectorBox>>

### [Text]
\<In this Demonstration, one mole of a binary mixture undergoes a batch distillation.\>

### [Text]
\<The first slider sets the mole fraction of the starting liquid. When you click the "click to collect" button, liquid evaporates and the distillate is collected in a collection flask. The amount collected into each flask is set with the second slider. The collection flask is then set aside and an empty flask is substituted.\>

### [Text]
\<The process repeats when you click the "click to collect button" again, until finally 0.2 mol remains in the still.\>

### [Text]
\<The equilibrium temperature is shown next to the thermometer.\>

### [Text]
Select either the collection flasks, an x-y plot, or a T-x-y plot to be displayed on the right.

### [Text]
Click "reset" to display one of the plots or to start over.

### [Text]
\<You can choose either an ideal solution (no azeotrope), a minimum-boiling temperature azeotrope or a maximum-temperature azeotrope with the drop-down menu.\>

### [Text]
\<Hovering the mouse over an object reveals further information about that object.\>

(*The total volume of the less volatile component collected into flasks.*)

### [Text]
In batch distillation of a binary mixture, a fixed amount of the feed mixture F (mol), with initial mole fraction Subscript[x, F], evaporates into a distillate collection flask; Subscript[D, i] (mol) is the mass of distillate collected into flask i, and Subscript[x, D, i] is the mole fraction within distillate flask i. This process is repeated to fill several distillate collection flasks. The overall mass balance is:

### [Text]
F=Subscript[B, final]+Subscript[D, total]						(1)

### [Text]
and the balance for the more volatile component is:

### [Text]
F Subscript[x, F]=Subscript[B, final]Subscript[x, B, final]+Subscript[D, total]Subscript[x, D, avg],				(2)

### [Text]
where Subscript[x, D, avg] is the average mole fraction within the distillate collection flasks and Subscript[B, final] and Subscript[x, B, final] are the final mass and mole fraction within the bottom (boiler) vessel. The value of Subscript[x, D, avg] can be calculated as:

### [Text]
Subscript[x, D, avg]=<<UnderoverscriptBox>>(Subscript[D, i]Subscript[x, D, i]+Subscript[D, i+1]Subscript[x, D, i+1]\[Ellipsis] Subscript[D, n]Subscript[x, D, n])/Subscript[D, total],		(3)

### [Text]
where the total number of collection stages is n (equivalent to the total number of distillate collection flasks). 

### [Text]
Total distillate is given by:

### [Text]
Subscript[D, total]=<<UnderoverscriptBox>>Subscript[D, i]+Subscript[D, i+1]\[Ellipsis] Subscript[D, n].					(4)

### [Text]
Because the saturated vapor is in thermodynamic equilibrium with the saturated liquid in the vessel (and there is only one equilibrium stage: the boiler/bottom vessel), the composition of the vapor Subscript[y, B] is a function of the composition in the bottom vessel. During evaporation, both compositions change with time, except when the composition of the liquid is an azeotrope. An equilibrium function is:

### [Text]
Subscript[y, B]=f(Subscript[x, B]).							(5)

### [Text]
Numerical approximations such as Antoine's equation are often used to model f(Subscript[x, B]) using experimental data. Other cases, such as constant relative volatility, may also be used to approximate this relationship.

### [Text]
Composition of Subscript[x, B, i+1] can be calculated by integration. Equation (5) must be substituted for Subscript[y, B].

### [Text]
Subscript[\[Integral],Subscript[B, i]]^Subscript[B, i+1](1)/(B)dB=Subscript[\[Integral],Subscript[x, B, i]]^Subscript[x, B, i+1](1)/(Subscript[y, B] - Subscript[x, B])d\[VeryThinSpace]Subscript[x, B].					(6)

### [Text]
Finally, a mass balance can be used to solve for the distillate composition, Subscript[x, D, i+1]:

### [Text]
Subscript[x, D, i+1]=(Subscript[B, i]Subscript[x, B,i]-Subscript[B, i+1]Subscript[x, B,i+1])/(Subscript[B, i]-Subscript[B, i+1]).					(7)
